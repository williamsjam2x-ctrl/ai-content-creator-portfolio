PROJECT 04 · EXPLAINER CONTENT SYSTEM

Stickman Explainer Blueprint

A reusable long-form video production template: topic discovery → researched script → timed scenes → visual prompts → thumbnail → quality checks.

Built for educational storytelling about ancient humans, survival, archaeology, biology, predators, animal behaviour and natural history. The system is designed for a US/Tier-1 audience while remaining globally understandable.

01 · PRODUCTION PIPELINE

How each episode is built

STEP 01

Topic & audience

Choose one strong question with clear curiosity, stakes and a meaningful payoff. Check audience fit before scripting.

STEP 02

Research & verify

Separate established facts, informed interpretation and uncertainty. Do not invent dates, evidence, animal behaviour or quotes.

STEP 03

Write the story

Open with a question or surprising situation, develop discoveries in logical order, and end with a payoff that answers the opening.

STEP 04

Time the scenes

Assign each scene a purpose, narration, visual evidence, estimated duration, transitions and sound notes.

STEP 05

Plan the visuals

Create one consistent image/video prompt per scene, preserving the Stickman design, location, props, direction and action continuity.

STEP 06

QA before release

Check facts, pacing, visual consistency, sound, spelling, title/thumbnail fit and export settings. Fix and retest before owner approval.

02 · SAMPLE SCRIPT TEMPLATE

Episode structure you can reuse

Working title: [A clear curiosity-driven question]

Audience: US / Tier-1, accessible worldwide

Format: YouTube long-form · 16:9 · calm American male explainer voice · 140–162 words per minute

Research status: Replace placeholders with verified sources before production.

[0:00–0:20] THE HOOK NARRATION: Imagine facing [specific survival problem] with only [verified limitation]. The surprising question is: how did [person/animal/group] manage to [central outcome]? VISUAL: One clear wide shot establishes the setting and immediate stakes. Keep the Stickman design locked. SOUND: Quiet environmental ambience; restrained low musical pulse. [0:20–1:00] THE CONTEXT NARRATION: First, we need to understand [essential background]. Evidence from [verified source/site/study] indicates [carefully stated fact]. What remains uncertain is [real uncertainty]. VISUAL: Simple map, timeline or diagram only where it improves understanding. SOUND: Light diegetic ambience; music stays below narration. [MAIN BODY] THREE DISCOVERIES DISCOVERY 1 — Establish the first obstacle and the evidence. DISCOVERY 2 — Reveal the strategy, mechanism or behavioural clue. DISCOVERY 3 — Explain the trade-off, danger or unexpected consequence. For every scene: state its purpose, write narration, show supporting visual evidence, add a transition and note its duration. [PAYOFF] ANSWER THE OPENING QUESTION NARRATION: So the answer is not simply [oversimplification]. The evidence points to [balanced conclusion], while [remaining caveat] still matters. VISUAL: Return to the opening image with one meaningful change that makes the discovery clear. [ENDING] ONE LAST INSIGHT NARRATION: The bigger lesson is [meaningful takeaway]. Which part would you like to explore next? VISUAL: Simple closing shot; leave room for end-screen elements.

This is a reusable template, not a finished fact-checked episode. Fill it with the selected topic, sources and evidence before publishing.

03 · SCENE-BY-SCENE TIMELINE

Production timeline template

SCENE 01 · HOOK · 0:00–0:20

Show the central problem

Narration: Ask the episode's core question; don't explain the whole answer immediately.

Visual prompt: Flat 2D Stickman with pale oval head, dark chunky hair and brown tunic; clear silhouette; one readable action; 16:9 composition; simple background; no text inside image.

Continuity: Record character position, props, environment and light direction.

SCENE 02 · CONTEXT · 0:20–1:00

Make the setting understandable

Narration: Give only the context needed to understand the stakes.

Visual prompt: Same locked character design; a clean 2D map or diagram supports the spoken fact; strong contrast and uncluttered layout.

Continuity: Label maps/diagrams in editing, and verify every factual label.

SCENE 03 · DISCOVERY · [TIME]

Reveal evidence

Narration: Explain what the evidence shows and what it cannot prove.

Visual prompt: A single clear action or visual metaphor, consistent with previous character proportions and palette.

Continuity: Match the previous scene's movement direction and object placement.

SCENE 04 · PAYOFF · [TIME]

Resolve the opening question

Narration: Deliver the answer, caveat and meaning.

Visual prompt: Return to the opening composition with a visible change that communicates the discovery.

Continuity: End with clean framing for the next beat or end screen.

04 · VISUAL PROMPT LOCK

Reusable image prompt

Create a clean flat 2D Stickman explainer illustration for a YouTube educational video, 16:9. Character lock: pale oval head, dark chunky hair, simple readable face, brown tunic, consistent body proportions and line weight. [Describe only the scene-specific action, setting, props and emotion.] Use clear silhouettes, deliberate composition, restrained colours and a simple background. Preserve continuity with the previous scene. The image must help explain the narration, not act as random decoration. No photorealism, no 3D, no anime, no watercolor, no extra fingers/limbs, no changed costume, no inconsistent character design, no generated lettering or watermark.

Thumbnail direction

One strong subject, a readable threat/discovery contrast, and 2–4 short words in yellow/white with a dark outline. Avoid clutter and do not promise a result the episode cannot support.

Voice and sound

Calm American male explainer delivery at roughly 140–162 words per minute. Voice-over adds point of view, context or mystery; it should not merely describe what the viewer already sees. Use diegetic ambience for the scene and non-diegetic music sparingly beneath narration.

05 · QUALITY GATE

Release checklist

  • ☐ One clear audience question and meaningful payoff.
  • ☐ Every material factual claim checked against a reliable source.
  • ☐ Uncertainty and interpretation are clearly distinguished from established fact.
  • ☐ Script length fits the target runtime at the selected speaking rate.
  • ☐ Every scene has narration purpose, duration, visual prompt and transition.
  • ☐ Stickman design remains consistent across all shots.
  • ☐ Music never masks narration; sound cues have a clear purpose.
  • ☐ Thumbnail/title accurately represent the episode.
  • ☐ Captions, spelling, audio, frame edges and export playback checked.
  • ☐ Final status: DRAFT → RESEARCHED → SCRIPT_READY → MEDIA_READY → EDITED → QA_RUNNING → QA_PASSED → READY_FOR_OWNER → OWNER_APPROVED → PUBLISHED.
