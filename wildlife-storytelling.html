CASE STUDY · WILDMIND STORIES

Wildlife stories built on evidence, not exaggeration.

My approach to animal behaviour and science explainers combines a strong story question with source checks, purposeful visuals and a final accuracy review.

Explore wildlife footage ↗Back to portfolio

Project goal

Make a wildlife or science topic clear, engaging and responsible for a general audience. The story should create curiosity without inventing animal thoughts, danger or scientific certainty.

Production workflow

01

Choose a specific question

Start with one question about an animal, behaviour or survival strategy. Define what the viewer should understand by the end.

02

Check the evidence

Use reliable scientific or conservation sources. Separate established findings from open questions. Keep source notes with the script.

03

Build a story sequence

Plan the hook, context, discoveries, evidence, explanation and payoff. Each scene should move the viewer forward.

04

Match visuals to claims

Use footage to show what it genuinely supports. Label illustrative or stock footage when it does not document the exact event being discussed.

05

Review before release

Check factual accuracy, narration, visual relevance, pacing, captions and source attribution before approval.

Quality checklist

  • Important factual claims have traceable sources.
  • Animal behaviour is not anthropomorphised as fact.
  • Visuals do not falsely imply they document the exact event.
  • Narration adds context instead of describing every visible action.
  • The ending answers the opening question or provides a clear takeaway.

Illustrative stock visual. It is not presented as original footage or proof of a specific behaviour.

Deliverables

  • Research notes and source list
  • Story outline and script beats
  • Scene-by-scene visual plan
  • Narration and sound notes
  • Accuracy and publication checklist
Core principle: the story earns attention through discovery. Evidence earns trust.
